Dear Rowan,

Thank you again for the documentation updates -- they made the next stage of
our work considerably faster. In return, three findings from using the data.
The first is the substantive one.

**1. Level 2 appears to remove GHI when the direct sensor is flagged.**

Working with BCO.radiation at PT10M over January 2024 to September 2026,
hourly completeness at our 90% sample threshold is 16,435 of 23,976 hours.
The shortfall is not scattered -- it falls in two contiguous runs, and both
coincide exactly with sw_dir_sensor_status = 3 in radiation_c2:

    2024-03-13 .. 2024-05-02     50.5 days
    2024-10-14 .. 2025-04-06    173.1 days

The direct-sensor measurements are clearly anomalous across those runs. On
2024-12-15, SWD_dir reaches 6,165 W/m2 with a daily mean of 4,546 including
overnight -- against a solar constant of roughly 1,361 W/m2.

On that same day, Level 1 SWD_global remains populated and shows a normal
diurnal pattern: near zero overnight, rising from mid-morning, hourly mean
peaking at 762 W/m2, instantaneous maximum 1,121.6.

We compared both flagged runs against three unflagged controls, one
deliberately covering the same October-to-March season, sampling roughly
200,000 points in each:

  period                     status        n      max  >1400  >1100  NaN
  2024-03-13 .. 2024-05-02        3  208,600   1489.8     11   2723    0
  2024-10-14 .. 2025-04-06        3  202,408   1533.9      1    806    0
  2024-05-03 .. 2024-10-14        0  201,818   1449.1      1   1134    0
  2025-04-07 .. 2025-09-30        0  201,806   1606.2      5   1702    0
  2025-10-01 .. 2026-03-31        0  200,308   1379.9      0    737    0

The highest maximum in the comparison occurs in a clean period, and the
flagged runs show no anomalous upper tail relative to the controls. Every
period has zero NaN and nothing below -10 W/m2.

That suggests roughly 224 days of potentially recoverable GHI in Level 1
being removed from Level 2 when the direct-sensor flag is active.

There may well be a physical reason for the exclusion that we cannot see from
outside. If the fault concerns the SOLYS2 tracker or its pointing rather than
the pyrheliometer alone, that would affect the direct measurement and also
the shaded pyranometer, and removing those would be right. Our question is
whether the unshaded SWD_global measurement is independent of that failure.

So: could you confirm what sw_dir_sensor_status = 3 represents operationally,
and whether it is intended to invalidate SWD_global as well as the direct and
diffuse components?

We have not tested calibration drift and are not claiming these observations
establish accuracy -- only that Level 1 GHI is populated and behaves
consistently with unflagged periods. If SWD_global does remain valid under
that condition, those periods would restore a substantial part of the 2024/25
dry season for our assessment.

**2. Two advertised aggregations do not appear to exist.**

The catalog offers time values of raw, PT1M, PT10M, PT1H, P1D for
BCO.radiation. The object store currently holds three:

    BCO.radiation_x255960_0.zarr/
    BCO.radiation_x255960_0_PT1M.zarr/
    BCO.radiation_x255960_0_PT10M.zarr/

PT1H and P1D return 404 on both allowed revisions. The example on the
radiation documentation page uses time="PT1H", and because the URL template
falls back rather than erroring, that request returns raw data -- which is
why the page reports 2.5 million records for a single month.

**3. The licence has three different answers.**

The PT10M dataset attribute reads license: CC0-1.0. Your email of 1 October
says CC BY. The portal pages we checked state neither for the radiation data
specifically. CC0 waives attribution and CC BY requires it, and we are
recording the licence in a formal assessment protocol -- could you confirm
which is authoritative for this dataset?

**One implementation note that may save someone an afternoon.** Your
documentation pins intake==2.0.8. Under 2.0.9 the Jinja conditional in the
URL template does not render, so parameterised requests silently fall back to
the raw store -- and opening that realises a 2.13 GiB time coordinate, which
killed our process twice before we traced it. We now open the stores by
explicit URL, which is also better provenance since the revision and build
are recorded.

**On your original question**, about filtering insufficiently sampled
intervals: our requirement differs from a climatological one. We score
weather forecasts against observations hour by hour, so a sampling gap and a
forecast error land in the same column and cannot be separated afterwards. We
need each reference hour to represent the whole hour.

The suggestion I would make is less about the threshold than about where it
lives. A rule that filters destroys the information a user would need to
choose differently; a rule that flags preserves it. raw_count and time_bounds
do this very well at ten minutes -- they were more useful to us than anything
in the documentation -- and the same principle would apply to the Level 2 QC,
where a per-sensor flag carried forward would let a downstream user judge
which variables a fault actually invalidates.
